# Infrared Sauna vs Traditional Sauna: What's the Difference?

Saunas have been part of wellness routines for centuries, but the sauna you'll find at a modern recovery studio often isn't the one you're picturing. Most new studios in Dubai, including Beit Sisu, use **infrared saunas** rather than traditional ones.

So what's actually the difference, and does it matter which one you choose? Here's a simple guide.

## How a traditional sauna works

A traditional sauna, the classic Finnish style, heats the **air** in the room, usually with an electric or wood-burning stove topped with stones. Pouring water over the stones creates bursts of steam.

Because the air itself is doing the heating, traditional saunas run hot, often somewhere around 80–100°C. That intense heat is part of the appeal for many people, but it can feel overwhelming, especially for beginners or anyone who doesn't tolerate heat well.

## How an infrared sauna works

An infrared sauna uses infrared heaters to warm **your body directly**, rather than heating all the air around you first. It's similar to the feeling of sunshine on your skin on a cool day: the air isn't especially hot, but you feel the warmth right away.

The result is a gentler, more comfortable heat. At Beit Sisu, our full-spectrum infrared saunas run **between 50 and 79°C**, and you still work up a proper sweat.

## The key differences at a glance

- **What gets heated:** traditional saunas heat the air; infrared saunas warm your body directly.
- **Temperature:** traditional saunas are often around 80–100°C; our infrared saunas run 50–79°C.
- **How it feels:** traditional heat is intense and steamy; infrared is a dry, gentler warmth many people find easier to relax in.
- **Session length:** because infrared is more comfortable, most people can stay in longer and get more out of each session.
- **Steam:** traditional saunas use water on hot stones for steam; infrared saunas are dry.

## Which one is better?

Neither is "better". They're different experiences. If you love intense heat and steam, a traditional sauna might be your thing. If you want to relax, unwind and sweat without feeling overwhelmed, infrared is a great place to start, and it's especially popular with first-timers.

Infrared also pairs perfectly with **cold plunging**. Because the heat is gentler, it's easy to alternate between the sauna and an ice bath several times in one session. That's the idea behind contrast therapy.

## Why people use infrared saunas

People come to infrared saunas for different reasons:

- **Relaxation and better sleep:** a session helps your body switch into its "rest" mode, and many guests sleep noticeably better afterwards.
- **Muscle recovery:** heat increases blood flow to tired muscles and helps ease soreness after training.
- **Skin:** sweating boosts circulation and leaves many people with a healthy glow.
- **Stress relief:** a quiet, private space to switch off, with no phone, no noise and no crowds.

## Who should check with a doctor first?

Saunas aren't suitable for everyone. Please speak to your doctor before booking if you're pregnant or breastfeeding, have a heart or cardiovascular condition, are prone to bleeding, or have any chronic health condition. Children under 13 aren't permitted, and guests aged 13–17 must be accompanied by a parent or guardian.

Drink plenty of water before, during and after your session.
